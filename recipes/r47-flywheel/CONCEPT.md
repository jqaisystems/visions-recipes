# R47 · Flywheel · concept

**Style (season 7, claymation, 1 of 3):** stop-motion clay made entirely in code. One WebGL2 raymarcher with smooth-blended signed distance fields, so any form can melt into any other without a cut, the thing real claymation does best. Thumbprints and tool marks are pressed into the surface, the surface shifts slightly on every pose the way hand-moved clay does, and the whole film is posed on twos at 12 poses a second over a 30 fps render. One soft key light, real soft shadows and a painted card backdrop. It shares the raymarch with season 3 but none of its look: no chrome, no liquid, no glass.

**Made for:** the Pinterest Growth Automation service (ai.joaoqueiros.com/services/pinterest-growth), which has no film yet. It isn't an internal tool, so the film ends with the service link.

**The idea:** the service page says Pinterest is "a flywheel, not a faucet". So the film is one clay machine, and everything in it is made from one lump. The lump is rolled and cut into a batch of pins, a person approves the batch once, and the pins are loaded onto a big heavy wheel. Each day the wheel clicks round one notch and sets one pin on a growing board. Nobody touches it. It starts slow and awkward, the days speed up, and the seasons pass behind it.

**Why it's a wow:** the craft is visible in every frame, and almost nobody makes this in code. Whole scenes melt into each other, words are pressed out of clay and squashed flat again, and a machine with real weight keeps turning.

**Beats** (about 32 s):
1. **One lump.** A plain lump drops into frame and settles with a heavy squash. A thumb-sized dent presses in, and it starts to move.
2. **The strategy.** It flattens and sprouts boards, which grow into labelled shelves. "A STRATEGY, THEN A BATCH."
3. **The batch.** A clay sheet rolls out and a cutter stamps pin after pin, fast, on the beat, until a hundred tall tiles stand in rows. "100+ PINS, DESIGNED ONCE."
4. **You approve once.** One huge stamp comes down on the whole batch. "YOU APPROVE THE BATCH, NOT EACH POST."
5. **The wheel.** The tiles fly up and load themselves onto a huge flywheel, which sags and groans with the weight. Stillness, one held breath.
6. **Day one.** One notch. One pin lands on an empty board. "DAY 1." Then day 2, day 3, each a clean click.
7. **It compounds.** The wheel gets faster and the days become a blur of notches. The backdrop melts through spring, summer, autumn and winter while the board fills and grows out of frame. "A FLYWHEEL, NOT A FAUCET."
8. **Nobody touches it.** The camera pulls back from the wheel turning alone in an empty room. "IT RUNS WHILE YOU DON'T."
9. **Close.** The whole machine slumps and melts back into the single lump, one last squash, and the logo lands flat with "Pinterest Growth Automation", the URL and a demo data label.

**Sound:** composed in code. Plucked and wooden, like a toy: marimba, woodblock and upright bass, with a soft wet squelch on each big clay move. The wheel's click is the tempo, so the music speeds up as the days do. One breath before day one.

**Look:** warm and handmade. Terracotta, mustard, cream and a dusty teal on a soft painted backdrop, in daylight.

**Honesty rules:**
- Facts come only from the service page: board strategy, a designed batch of typically 100+ pins, daily posting from a queue, approving the batch and not each post, a pipeline that keeps running, "a flywheel, not a faucet", reach compounding over weeks and months.
- No views, followers, saves, sales or any result. The wheel shows how it runs, never what it earned. The board filling up means pins posted, not reach, and no chart goes up.
- No client is named, and no client's numbers appear.
- All pins are invented, blank or simple clay patterns, labelled demo data. No real brand.
- Generic labels only: no hosting, scheduling or code-host product names on screen, and no third-party logos. "Pinterest" appears only in the service's name, and its logo is never used.
- "It runs while you don't" must not suggest it needs nothing: designing and approving the batch comes first, as the film shows, and the page says it needs topping up with fresh pins or a managed plan.

**How the film differs from the first idea:** the count stops at day 75 of a 108 pin batch, so the wheel still carries pins when it runs alone. The count stands on a still clay dial on the axle, because it couldn't be read against the turning spokes. The board doesn't literally grow out of frame: the camera pushes in on it instead. The end card is flat on dark, because the logo can't sit on a light ground, so the lump swells to fill the frame first.
