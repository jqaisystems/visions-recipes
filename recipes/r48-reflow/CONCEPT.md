# R48 · Reflow · concept

**Style (season 7, claymation, 2 of 3):** stop-motion clay made entirely in code, on the R47 raymarcher with its thumbprints and 12 poses a second. It has to look like a different film from Flywheel: saturated plasticine in cobalt, tomato and butter on a dusky lilac backdrop, a lower, stronger evening light with long shadows, and a camera that moves in and around the forms rather than watching a machine from the front.

**Made for:** the AI-Powered Web Design service (ai.joaoqueiros.com/services/web-design), which has only an 8-second clip of its own. It sells a service, so the film ends with the service link.

**The idea:** a website is the one thing people assume is rigid, and a good responsive one isn't. So the film builds a whole website out of soft clay and then squeezes it. Two walls close in, the page buckles, and its three columns slump into two and then one, in a single take with no cut. "Reflow" is the web term for what a layout does when its screen changes, and it's also what clay does.

**Why it's a wow:** a layout physically reflowing under pressure is an image I haven't seen, and it only works because any clay form here can melt into any other. It also explains responsive design to someone who has never heard the word.

**Beats** (about 32 s):
1. **The brand.** Three lumps of colour drop in, one by one, with a heavy squash. "YOU BRING THE BRAND."
2. **Not a template.** A mould stamps out a row of identical grey sites. One lump lands on the end and they all topple like dominoes. "NOT A TEMPLATE."
3. **Wireframes first.** Grey clay coils lay themselves out on a slab, drawing a page: boxes, lines and a nav. "WIREFRAMES FIRST."
4. **The page.** The colour lumps melt across the slab and the coils swell into a finished page: a nav, a hero with "YOUR BRAND" raised in clay, and three cards. "HAND-CODED, WITH AI ASSISTANCE."
5. **The squeeze (the showpiece).** One held breath. Two huge walls slam in from the sides. The page buckles and bulges, the cards slump from three columns to two, then one, and it stands tall as a phone. "DESKTOP. TABLET. MOBILE." The walls release and it springs back with a wobble.
6. **The real thing.** A clay cursor presses a clay button, and the button squashes and springs. "REVIEW THE REAL SITE, NOT A MOCK-UP."
7. **The plumbing.** The slab turns round to show its back, where clay pipes run to plates labelled SCHEMA, SITEMAP and META. "THE SEO PLUMBING IS INCLUDED."
8. **Yours.** The whole site folds shut into one clay folder and slides towards the camera. "EVERY FILE IS YOURS." Then the logo lands flat, with "AI-Powered Web Design", the URL and a demo label.

**Sound:** composed in code and bouncy: pizzicato strings, a tuba, hand claps and a slide whistle for the squeeze, with a wet squelch on each column change. The dominoes are a run of woodblock clicks that speeds up as they fall. One breath before the walls.

**Look:** cobalt, tomato and butter with a dark plum, on dusky lilac. Archivo at 900 as extruded clay. The end card is flat on dark, as the logo needs.

**Honesty rules:**
- Facts come only from the service page: you provide the brand, wireframes before visual design, not a template or site builder, hand-coded with AI assistance, works on mobile, tablet and desktop, you review the real responsive site and not static mockups, an SEO baseline (meta tags, schema, sitemap), you own every file.
- No speed claims, no prices, no page speed or search scores, and no results.
- "Hand-coded" always comes with "with AI assistance", as the page says it, so nobody thinks AI wasn't involved.
- The site on screen says only "YOUR BRAND", so it can't be mistaken for a client's. It's labelled demo, and no client sites appear.
- No third-party names or logos. The AI tools the page names, and the site builders it compares against, stay off screen. The grey template sites carry no marks.

**How the film differs from the first idea:** the slab itself turns round to show the plumbing, because the painted card behind it leaves the camera no room to go round. TABLET and MOBILE stand on the left wall and leave with it. The backdrop goes grey for the template and the wireframe, so the colour arrives with the brand, then dark blue behind the site, then dusk for the folder. The site shrinks into the folder before the flap shuts.
