# R49 · Mould · concept

**Style (season 7, claymation, 3 of 3, the finale):** stop-motion clay made entirely in code, on the R47 and R48 raymarcher with its thumbprints and 12 poses a second. Two things are new. The clay is marbled: colour runs through its volume and really twists as the clay is kneaded. And the pictures are relief plaques, so one wall can hold dozens of different images.

**Made for:** the AI Brand Visuals service (ai.joaoqueiros.com/services/brand-visuals). This film sells a service, so it ends with the service link.

**The idea:** the page says the style system "is why every image looks like part of the same campaign". A style system is a mould. So the film squashes a pile of mismatched pictures into one lump, builds a mould from the four parts of the system, and presses out a whole campaign that clearly belongs together. Then the client is handed the mould.

**Why it's a wow:** the showpiece is the press running on the beat until the camera pulls back to a wall of different pictures that all share one look. Then one wrong picture appears in that wall, in hot pink, and a huge DO-NOT stamp squashes it flat. That gag explains a do-not list faster than any sentence could.

**Beats** (about 32 s):
1. **The mismatch.** Black studio, one spotlight. Clay pictures drop in, each in its own clashing colours and shapes. "ONE BRAND?" They wobble and don't belong together.
2. **The lump.** Two hands' worth of pressure: the pictures are squashed together and kneaded into one ball. The colours twist into marbling as it turns. "NOT A DEFAULT AI IMAGE."
3. **The mould.** Four heavy plates land one by one and lock together, each stamped: REFERENCES, RECIPES, RULES, DO-NOT. "A STYLE SYSTEM FOR YOUR BRAND." The marbling settles into one palette.
4. **The first set.** The press comes down once, slowly, and six plaques slide out. A clay thumb presses a mark into them. "YOU APPROVE THE FIRST SET."
5. **The run (the showpiece).** The press slams on the beat, plaques fly out and land on a growing wall: a boat, a bird, mountains over a lake, a car, a yacht, a figure with a megaphone. The camera pulls all the way back. "ONE LOOK."
6. **The do-not.** One hot pink plaque sits in the wall. The music stops. A huge stamp drops and flattens it, and the gap fills with a plaque that fits. "THAT'S WHAT THE DO-NOT LIST IS FOR."
7. **It moves.** The plaques come alive on twos: the boat rocks, the bird flaps, the lake ripples, and a clay record starts to spin. "IMAGES. MOTION. MUSIC."
8. **Yours.** The mould lifts off the press and slides across the table to the camera until it fills the frame. "THE SYSTEM IS YOURS TO KEEP." Then the logo lands flat, with "AI Brand Visuals", the URL and a demo label.

**Sound:** composed in code, late-night and physical: upright bass, brushed drums and vibraphone, with the press as the kick and a wet slap on each plaque. Full stop for the pink plaque, one stamp, then the band comes back. It should sound nothing like Flywheel or Reflow.

**Look:** a black box studio under one hard, warm spotlight, with a slight sheen on the clay. Ember, bone and deep teal once the system is on; clashing colours before it. Hot pink belongs only to the wrong plaque. Archivo at 900 as extruded clay. The end card is flat on dark, as the logo needs.

**Honesty rules:**
- Facts come only from the service page: on-brand images, motion and music made with AI by a brand designer; the style system's four parts; a first set of six to eight images approved before the full batch; you keep the system.
- The film is code and the service makes AI visuals, so nobody should think these clay pictures are what they get. A label stays on screen: "AI BRAND VISUALS · DEMO PICTURES". The end card says "A SERVICE · THIS FILM IS CODE".
- "Yours to keep" is about the style system only. It never implies owning or having copyright in every image, because the page's own FAQ says purely AI work may not be protected.
- "One look" is about what the system is for. No results, prices, kit counts or claims about any client. No client work appears.
- No third-party names or logos. The car, yacht and other plaques are generic shapes with no marks,.

**How the film differs from the first idea:** plates slide onto the screw from alternate sides and don't drop from above, because the screw is in the way. The stamp has no handle: its back faces the camera and carries DO-NOT, so it reads as it slams down, then lifts past the camera. All but three pictures sink back into the wall for "IMAGES. MOTION. MUSIC.", so those three read clearly. The arm comes in from the right, and the words sit close to the wall so their shadows read as relief.
