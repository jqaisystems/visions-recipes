# R22 · Zoetrope · concept

**Style (reusable):** Zoetrope. The oldest machines made to make pictures move, built in 3D and lit by a strobe:
each one is a spinning blur until the strobe locks to its turn, and then the pictures on it come alive. Anyone can
load their own clips onto the drums.

**Why now:** strobe-lit spinning sculptures are some of the most shared motion clips there are: a blur that snaps
into a living animation when the flash matches the spin. Analogue machines playing new AI imagery is also a popular
direction. The twist here: the machines and the strobe are code, and the pictures are the loops from the Motion page.

**Made for:** the Motion page of Imagined Worlds (visions.joaoqueiros.com/motion), João Queirós' cinematic loops made
with Midjourney video. Each machine plays one loop. No loop count on screen.

1. **Spin.** In a dark cabinet a lacquered drum spins under a warm lamp, its painted numerals smeared into streaks.
2. **Lock.** A strobe stutters on and locks to the spin: the drum stands still and a thermal horse gallops inside it,
   the same subject that started moving pictures.
3. **The dive.** The camera drops into the drum and through the far picture, into the loop itself.
4. **The cabinet.** A praxinoscope (a ballerina in its mirrors), a phenakistiscope (a ring of kingfishers) and a flip
   book (a child running in dusty ruins), each with a brass plaque, then all four in one wide shot.
5. **Out of phase.** The strobe drifts off the beat, stops, everything smears and winds down, then one flash locks it.
6. **Title.** A last flash into the burning carousel loop: "Motion", "Worlds that refused to hold still." and the URL.

**As built:** each loop plays 60 frames on an atlas, the stretch whose end best matches its start, cross-faded into a
loop; locked frames are one frozen render and unlocked frames are eight renders averaged into motion blur; the score
is composed in code from the same table, at 150 bpm, one beat per turn of the drum.

**What the open source leaves out:** nothing of the code. The loops ship as atlases and two small clips so the film
runs, and they are not licensed.
