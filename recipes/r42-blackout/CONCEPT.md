# R42 · Blackout · concept

**Style (season 6, ink feedback, 2 of 3):** wet ink bleeding through paper. Same family as R39 Echo, a page that keeps its own history, but the opposite film at a glance.
- Echo is pale and quiet: thin lines of ink that fade. Blackout floods: ink soaks through the sheet until the frame is black, and the picture is what the ink left behind.
- One WebGL2 feedback buffer holds two layers, ink and water. Water carries ink along a paper fibre texture, so edges feather like real ink on newsprint instead of blurring.
- The page restarts from clean paper at fixed anchors, so any frame can be rebuilt the same way (as in Echo).
- Crisp kinetic type sits on a 2D layer above the ink.

**Made for:** Codex Control Center (ai.joaoqueiros.com/systems/codex-control-center), João's open-source local dashboard for Codex.

**The idea:** the system's promise is that it keeps the record and not the words. So the film is blackout poetry. A session log fills the page, ink floods over everything private, and what survives is only the metadata: times, event types, tool names. Those survivors become the dashboard.

**Look:** newsprint bone #EDE8DC, printing ink #0C0C0E, and one vermilion #E8341C reserved for the stamp and nothing else. Heavy condensed grotesque for full-frame type, a mono face for the log. No gradients or glow.

**Beats** (about 34 s):
1. **The log.** A bone page. A fictional session log races up the frame, dense and readable: prompts, full paths, raw output, a fake token. "Every session leaves a trail."
2. **Blackout: the wow moment.** On the kick, a wet bar slams over each private line. The bars bleed, feather through the fibres and join, until the whole frame has gone black in one long soak. Only the times, event types and tool names remain, as bone cut-outs in the ink. "Metadata only."
3. **Observe.** The survivors slide into place and assemble the dashboard in negative: activity, usage, readiness, skills, with workspace labels instead of paths. "No API key to observe."
4. **Approve.** Task cards drop into a queue and wait. A huge vermilion APPROVE stamp slams, its splash ripples through the wet page, and one task runs, labelled "read-only". A full-access card arrives and gets stamped BLOCKED. "Nothing runs until you say so."
5. **The wash.** Publish check: water sweeps the frame. Fake credentials, logs, raw sessions and paths dissolve and run off the page, while source, fake fixtures and docs stay. The black rinses back to bone. "Checked before it's public."
6. **Close.** One drop lands and blooms to fill the frame. The official JQ AI SYSTEMS logo, placed flat, with "Codex Control Center", the URL and "Open source · MIT".

**Sound:** composed in code. A fast typewriter and teletype rhythm for the log, a wet slap per bar, one long descending soak into a sub drop for the blackout, a hard stamp with a splash for APPROVE, a dull thud for BLOCKED, a rush of water for the wash, and a resolving chord on the logo.

**Honesty rules:**
- Facts come only from the case study: metadata only, no API key for observation, read-only default, approval gates, full-access blocked in v1, publishing check, MIT and public repo.
- Everything shown is fictional demo data, including the fake token and paths. Nothing from a real session or vault appears.
- No results, no users and no time saved.
- "Codex" appears only as part of the system's own name. No other company or product names or logos.
- The film shows the checklist idea and must not imply that the scan guarantees safety.

**How the film differs from the first idea:**
- The survivors are islands of bare paper with black type, as in real blackout poetry, not bone letters cut out of the ink. The private words in the publish check are the ones cut out of the ink, so they melt when the water reaches them.
- The stamp lands on the task card and moves with it. Only its splash is on the wet page.
- Fonts: Archivo (condensed, 900) and JetBrains Mono.
